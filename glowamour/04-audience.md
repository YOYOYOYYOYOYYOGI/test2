# 04 — Target Audience Research

## How to read this file

Every statement is labelled:

- **[R] Researched** — grounded in published data, industry reporting, regulatory findings, or evidence found in your own repository.
- **[H] Hypothesis** — my inference from category knowledge and your specific situation. **Must be tested before you spend money on it.**
- **[T] Test method** — the cheapest way to find out whether the hypothesis is true.

I have deliberately avoided stereotyping. A 22-year-old student in Surat and a 45-year-old doctor's wife in Ahmedabad are not "the same Gujarati woman," and content that assumes they are will miss both. Segments below are **behavioural**, not demographic boxes.

**The honest framing:** audience research for a brand with no live sales history is mostly structured guessing plus a fast feedback loop. Your advantage is that your existing WhatsApp-order tool means **every order is a data point you already capture** — name, city, state, pincode, product, quantity, payment status, delivery charge. After 50 orders you will know more about your real customers than any research report. Plan to re-read this file at order #50 and rewrite it with facts.

**Who:** Women ~22–45, Ahmedabad / Surat / Vadodara / Rajkot / Gandhinagar and smaller Gujarat towns. Comfortable buying on Instagram, but they complete the purchase **in a WhatsApp conversation**, not on a checkout page. Family-oriented, price-aware but not price-obsessed.

- **[R]** Your own order data model is built for exactly this: WhatsApp clipboard parsing (`Customer / Phone / Product xN / Paid`), sample customers "Rahul Patel, Ahmedabad" and "Priya Shah" with COD, and state-based delivery rules keyed on Gujarat. You have already designed your operations around this buyer.
- **[R]** India-wide, WhatsApp is the default small-business commerce conversation layer; WhatsApp Pay (UPI) is live natively in India, and WhatsApp Business catalogues let a customer browse and cart without leaving the chat.
- **[H]** She will not pay a stranger ₹499 online without either COD or a conversation first.
- **[T]** Add a WhatsApp chip with a pre-filled message and count how many orders start there versus how many start with "DM GLOW." Two weeks of data settles it.

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Skincare interests** | Glow and "fresh-looking" skin; kesar/haldi/chandan traditions she already trusts from home use; simple 2–3 step routines she will actually follow; products that survive Gujarat heat and humidity; hard-water damage to skin and hair |
| **Questions before buying** | "Will it actually reach Surat?" · "COD છે?" · "Is it safe, will it suit my skin?" · "કઈ કંપની છે આ?" (which company is this?) · "What's the price including delivery?" · "Can I talk to someone first?" · "Is it sticky in summer?" · "શેનું બનેલું છે?" (what's it made of?) |

## Segment 2 — The ingredient-aware urban shopper

**Who:** 24–38, metro or tier-1 (Ahmedabad, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi NCR, Pune). Reads INCI lists. Already buys from Minimalist, The Derma Co, Dot & Key, Re'equil, Foxtale. Follows skincare-education creators. Skeptical by default and proud of it.

- **[R]** This is a large, well-documented Indian D2C segment; the current market structure has active-first brands (Minimalist — single actives at stated percentages, ₹300–600), sensorial multi-benefit brands (Dot & Key, ₹400–800), barrier-first derm-positioned brands (Re'equil), and mass natural brands (Mamaearth, Biotique, Himalaya).
- **[R]** ASCI's FY26 reporting found that in personal care the most common upheld violations were *"visible, rapid or guaranteed results"*, impossible timelines ("dandruff gone in 1 wash"), and manufactured scientific precision ("11.7x stronger hair"). **This segment knows that and is actively looking for brands that don't do it.**
- **[H]** She will pay ₹499 from an unknown brand **only** if the brand publishes percentages, full ingredients, and realistic timelines.
- **[T]** Post one carousel that is *only* your ingredient list with percentages and honest limits. Measure saves and sends versus your average. If it outperforms, this segment is reachable.

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Interests** | Percentages; pH; formulation elegance; niacinamide/arbutin/HA/panthenol; sunscreen as non-negotiable; "what does this actually do at this concentration"; price-per-gram; whether the brand is honest about limitations |
| **Questions before buying** | "What % niacinamide?" · "What's the pH?" · "Is the arbutin stabilised, and is hydroquinone at trace levels?" · "Who manufactures it?" · "Is it airless packaging?" · "Has it been stability-tested?" · "What's the full INCI?" · "Why is it ₹499 and not ₹1,200?" |
| **Problems** | Uneven-looking tone; dullness; barrier damage from over-exfoliating; confusion from contradictory advice; distrust of exaggerated claims |
| **Useful content** | Ingredient deep-dives with citations · "how to read an INCI list" · honest timelines (6–12 weeks for tone appearance, **with sunscreen**) · formulation decisions explained ("why we did not use pure vitamin C") · comparisons by ingredient, not by brand-bashing |
| **Why she hesitates** | Unknown brand + no published testing data = assumes it is a repackaged white-label product. She has been burned before |
| **What helps her decide** | Published percentages · full INCI · manufacturer disclosed · licence number · a stability or challenge test report · a founder who answers technical questions accurately · explicit statements of what the product **cannot** do |

⭐ **This segment is your unfair advantage — and you already have the ammunition.** `formulation/day-cream-v1-india-dossier.md` contains peer-reviewed citations (Bissett 2004, Hakozaki 2002 on niacinamide and melanosome transfer; SCCS/1642/22 on arbutin ≤2% in face creams), a reasoned exclusion list (no hydroquinone — Schedule H in India since the 2019 amendment; no steroids; no mercury; why pure L-ascorbic acid is not home-stable), pH windows, and a realistic outcome table. **Almost no small Indian Instagram skincare brand can produce that.** Turning even 10% of it into public content would make you the most credible small brand in your category overnight. Ask me to convert the dossier into a claims-substantiation sheet and a 20-part educational series.

## Segment 3 — The bride, the wedding household, and the kesar tradition

**Who:** Brides-to-be, their mothers and sisters, and anyone buying for a wedding household. Gujarat has an exceptionally dense wedding economy, and the season runs roughly November–February.

- **[R]** Kesar (saffron) is culturally embedded in Indian wedding and ceremony ritual — applied at functions, gifted, associated with auspiciousness and "glow." A product literally named **Kesar Goti Soap** inherits that association for free, with no claim needed.
- **[R]** Pre-bridal and bridal skincare is an established, high-spend search category in India.
- **[H]** Wedding purchases are **gifting** purchases — bought by one person for another, often in multiples, with less price sensitivity and much higher urgency.
- **[T]** In October–November, post one "wedding-season gifting" carousel and one kesar-ritual Reel. Measure DM volume versus your baseline.

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Interests** | Pre-bridal routines; "glow" for photographs; kesar/haldi rituals; gifting that looks thoughtful; products safe to start 6–8 weeks before the wedding; matching sets |
| **Questions** | "How long before the wedding should I start?" · "Will it cause a breakout at the worst time?" · "Is it safe to use during all the ceremonies?" · "Do you have a gift box?" · "Can it reach [town] by [date]?" |
| **Problems** | Time pressure; fear of a reaction before a photographed event; dullness and fatigue from wedding preparations; finding a gift that is not generic |
| **Useful content** | A realistic 8-week pre-bridal timeline (**with a patch-test warning and an explicit "do not try anything new in the last 2 weeks"** — that advice alone is worth following you for); kesar ritual content; gift-box options; "what a night cream can and cannot do before a wedding" |
| **Why she hesitates** | **Fear of a reaction is the dominant objection** — she has one date and cannot risk it. Also delivery-date anxiety |
| **What helps her decide** | Patch-test instructions given *proactively*; honest timelines; a stated dispatch date; gift packaging; a clear "if it reacts, here's what we do" policy |

⚠️ **Never promise a bridal glow by a date.** That is a guaranteed-results claim with an impossible timeline — precisely what ASCI upholds complaints against. Say: *"Start 8 weeks early. Patch test first. Realistically, hydration and a fresher look in days; tone appearance takes 6–12 weeks."* The honesty is the selling point.

## Segment 4 — The value-conscious tier-2/tier-3 buyer

**Who:** 20–40, smaller Gujarat towns and beyond (Junagadh, Bhuj, Nadiad, Anand, Palanpur, Bharuch) and other states' tier-2/3 cities. Lower skincare spend, higher fraud anxiety, mobile-only.

- **[R]** COD remains essential for tier-2/3 Indian e-commerce. Reported COD RTO is ~26% at the ₹150–300 price point, costing ₹150–300 per failed order — at ₹499 your exposure is real but manageable, especially with your existing state-based delivery rules.
- **[R]** Indian D2C skincare has been explicitly targeting tier-II and tier-III markets as a growth strategy (Dot & Key's stated approach from launch).
- **[H]** She is more influenced by a **conversation** than by any content. If she can ask one question and get a warm human answer, she converts at a higher rate than the urban shopper.
- **[T]** Track close rate (orders ÷ conversations started) separately for tier-2 pincodes versus Ahmedabad. Your Google Sheets already stores pincode and state — you can compute this today.

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Interests** | Value for money; multi-purpose products; family-safe; visible quality of packaging; long-lasting products |
| **Questions** | "Kitna padega total?" (what's the total, including delivery?) · "Paise kaise doon?" · "Asli hai na?" (is it genuine?) · "Wapas kar sakte hain?" · "Kitne din mein aayega?" |
| **Problems** | Limited budget; distrust of online payments; skin concerns she has no vocabulary for; products that don't arrive |
| **Useful content** | Total-cost-transparent posts (product + delivery = one number); simple routines with 2 products; "how to spot a genuine product" education; voice-note replies in Gujarati/Hindi |
| **Why she hesitates** | **Fraud fear is the single biggest barrier.** She has seen or heard of Instagram sellers who take UPI payment and disappear |
| **What helps her decide** | **COD** — removes almost all risk · a real person's face and name · a phone/WhatsApp she can call · other customers from her own town · a visible address · a GST number |

**Operational note:** given ~26% RTO risk on COD, add a **WhatsApp YES/NO order confirmation within 30 minutes of a COD order**. Industry reporting suggests this cuts RTO substantially. Your existing tool's duplicate-detection and phone-validation logic is a good foundation to build that habit on top of.

## Cross-segment: the questions every one of them asks

These seven questions appear in every segment. Answer each one **publicly and permanently** — in Highlights, in pinned posts, in captions — and you will remove most of the friction from every sale you ever make.

1. **"Is it genuine / is this a real business?"** → founder face, city, licence number, GST, real batch photos
2. **"Will it suit my skin?"** → honest "who this is for / who this is NOT for", patch-test instructions
3. **"What exactly is in it?"** → full INCI, percentages
4. **"How much, total, delivered?"** → published price + delivery charge by state
5. **"How do I pay, and is COD available?"** → COD + UPI, both stated everywhere
6. **"When will it reach me?"** → dispatch time + delivery time by region
7. **"What if something goes wrong?"** → written return/replacement policy, and one visible example of you honouring it

## How to test segments properly (instead of guessing forever)

You do not need a research budget. You need four cheap loops:

| Loop | How | Cost | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Trial Reels** | Once you pass **200 followers on a professional account** (Help Center threshold), publish test Reels to **non-followers only**. Compare hooks, segments and languages without polluting your grid. Insights after ~24 h. | Free | Which segment and which hook actually earns attention from strangers. Note: requires your account to be **eligible for recommendations** |
| **2. Story polls** | Weekly: "Night cream કે gel?" · "COD કે UPI?" · "તમે કયા શહેરમાંથી છો?" | Free | Self-declared preferences and geography, from people already warm |
| **3. DM logs** ⭐ | For 30 days, log the **exact first sentence** every inquirer sends you, plus their city. 20 rows is enough to see a pattern | Free | The single most accurate audience data available to you. Their words are your future captions |
| **4. Your own order sheet** | You already store name, city, state, pincode, product, qty, payment status, delivery charge. Add three columns: `Source (which Reel/post)`, `First question asked`, `Repeat? Y/N` | Free | Real buyer geography, real AOV, real repeat rate — the data that should replace this file at order #50 |

**Decision rule:** when a hypothesis in this file conflicts with 30 days of your own DM or order data, **the data wins, and you should rewrite this file.** Tell me what you find and I will revise the segments.
