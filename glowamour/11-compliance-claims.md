# 11 — Compliance & Claims Guide for Indian Cosmetics Marketing

**Read this before your first post.** This is the file that keeps you out of trouble, and it is also the file that makes you more interesting than your competitors — because almost every claim you are forbidden to make is a claim your competitors are making loudly.

> ⚠️ **I am not a lawyer.** This is a marketing-practice summary based on published regulatory and self-regulatory material. For anything involving your licence, your labels, or a claim you are unsure about, consult a regulatory professional or your state licensing authority. Nothing here is legal advice.

---

## Part 1 — The four regimes that apply to you

| Regime | What it governs | Why you should care |
|---|---|---|
| **Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 + Cosmetics Rules 2020** (CDSCO centrally, State Licensing Authorities for manufacturing) | Manufacture, sale, labelling, and claims of cosmetics in India | Manufacturing cosmetics for sale **without a licence is an offence**. Labelling requirements are mandatory, not optional. Your own dossier §9.8 says this plainly |
| **BIS IS 4707** (Part 1 & 2; Part 2 and a new Part 3 revised/published Aug–Sep 2025) | Classification of raw materials; prohibited and restricted substances; allowed preservatives | Determines what may legally be in your formula |
| **Consumer Protection Act 2019 + CCPA Guidelines on Misleading Advertisements (2022) + Greenwashing Guidelines (2024)** | "Misleading advertisement" = falsely describing a product, giving **unsubstantiated guarantees**, or deliberately concealing important information | The CCPA has **fined companies** for unsubstantiated whitening claims and warned of **₹50 lakh penalties for repeats**. A disclaimer in small print does not save an unsupportable headline claim |
| **ASCI Code** (self-regulatory) | Advertising content, including social media and influencer posts | **Chapter I: every objective claim must be capable of substantiation, produced on demand.** ASCI has dedicated guidelines for **skin-lightening/fairness** advertising and for **environmental/green** claims |

### Why this matters more for you than for most brands
- ASCI's FY26 reporting found **digital platforms accounted for ~97.3% of all ads scrutinised**, **82% of those being sponsored social content**, and **Meta platforms accounted for ~79.84% of digital violations**. Instagram is where enforcement attention is.
- **Personal care (16.9%)** was among the top influencer violation categories.
- Their summer-advertising review found **39 of 40 ads examined required modification or withdrawal**, with **sun-protection, tan-removal and cooling claims** among the biggest concerns.
- Their FY26 Annual Complaints Report lists exaggerated and unsubstantiated claims as headline problem categories.
- Research cited by legal analysts suggests only ~18% of cosmetic advertising claims are broadly trustworthy.

**Translation:** the enforcement environment is aimed exactly at the category and channel you are entering. Being scrupulous is not a handicap — it is a defensible position.

## Part 2 — Banned claims (never publish these, anywhere)

Anywhere = bio, caption, on-screen text, spoken audio, Story, Highlight, Reel cover, WhatsApp catalogue, ad copy, packaging.

### Absolute prohibitions
| ❌ Claim | Why |
|---|---|
| **Fairness, whitening, skin lightening, bleaching, "gori skin", "rang goro", "instant fairness", "permanent whitening"** | ASCI has dedicated fairness guidelines: advertising must not communicate discrimination based on skin colour, must not portray darker skin as unattractive/unhappy/inferior/unsuccessful, must not associate skin colour with socio-economic strata, caste, community, religion, profession or ethnicity, and must not perpetuate gender-based discrimination on the basis of skin colour. CCPA has fined brands for unsubstantiated whitening claims. **Meta's own ad policy prohibits permanent skin whitening/bleaching claims and "statements of inferiority about physical appearance."** |
| **Treats / cures / prevents acne, pimples, melasma, eczema, psoriasis, rosacea, infection** | Claiming to treat a disease makes your product a **drug**, not a cosmetic, under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act. Your own dossier §9.8: *"It does not treat, cure or prevent any disease, including acne vulgaris, melasma or post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation."* |
| **Anti-ageing as a medical outcome; "reverses ageing"; "rejuvenation"** | Schedule J of the Drugs Rules lists premature ageing, skin fairness and rejuvenation among prohibited advertising territory. Also falls under the **Drugs & Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act 1954** if framed as a remedy |
| **"100% safe", "no side effects", "completely harmless", "suitable for everyone"** | Your dossier: *"No cosmetic product is '100% side-effect free.' Any topical product can cause irritation or allergy in some individuals."* An absolute safety claim is an unsupportable guarantee under COPRA s.2(28) |
| **"Guaranteed results", "sure-shot", "100% effective"** | Unsubstantiated guarantee — squarely a misleading advertisement |
| **Fixed timelines: "results in 7 days", "glow in 3 days", "dandruff gone in 1 wash", "dark spots gone in 2 weeks"** | ASCI's most-commonly-upheld personal-care violation is *"visible, rapid or guaranteed results"* and *"impossible timelines"* |
| **Manufactured precision: "11.7× stronger", "5× more effective"** (without a comparative test) | Specifically named by ASCI as a violation pattern |
| **Superlatives: "India's No. 1", "best night cream", "world's finest"** | Needs substantiation; specifically flagged by ASCI in personal care |
| **"Dermatologist tested / recommended / approved"** without an actual report | The COPRA Guidelines include a direct illustration: a skin-cream ad claiming *"dermatologists recommend this product for all skin types"* **cannot be saved by a small-print "individual results may vary"** disclaimer if no actual recommendation existed |
| **"Clinically proven"** without a clinical study | Requires credible, current, methodologically sound evidence specific to the claim |
| **"100% natural", "chemical-free", "pure", "no chemicals"** | Water is a chemical. "Natural" is a substantiation-required absolute claim; ASCI's greenwashing guidelines and the CCPA 2024 Guidelines target exactly this. Your dossier §8.2: *"natural does not automatically mean safer"* |
| **"Organic"** without certification | Requires accredited certification |
| **"Ayurvedic", "herbal", "siddha"** without an AYUSH manufacturing licence | Implies a regulated product category and licensing you do not hold |
| **"Cruelty-free", "vegan"** without documentation | ASCI greenwashing guidelines require such claims to be truthfully verifiable and adequately disclosed |
| **"Eco-friendly", "sustainable", "planet friendly"** without robust data or recognised accreditation | ASCI's Jan 2024 guidelines: absolute environmental claims **cannot be diluted by a disclaimer, QR code or website link** |
| **Invented testimonials, fake reviews, purchased star ratings, fabricated before/afters** | Fraud. Also, on Instagram: fabricated content destroys the trust you are building, and AI-generated before/afters are invented evidence |
| **Undisclosed paid promotion** | ASCI requires advertisements on social media to be **clearly identifiable as promos/ads**. Influencer content must disclose the relationship |
| **"Tan removal"** as a promised outcome | Among the biggest problem areas in ASCI's 2026 summer-ad review |

### Also avoid
- Any framing that makes a person feel **inferior about their appearance** — prohibited by Meta's ad policy and against ASCI's decency and fairness chapters.
- **Denigrating a named competitor.** ASCI requires comparisons to be factual, accurate, capable of substantiation, and prohibits unfairly attacking or discrediting another product, even by implication.
- Using a **healthcare professional in a promotional endorsement capacity** for cosmetic/wellness products (ASCI healthcare guidelines §3).

## Part 3 — Safe claims (the vocabulary to use instead)

The pattern: **cosmetic-appearance language, hydration language, sensory language, and honest limitation language.** All of these are defensible because they describe what a cosmetic actually does.

| ❌ Instead of | ✅ Say |
|---|---|
| "Whitens skin" | "Helps your skin **look** brighter and more radiant" |
| "Removes dark spots" | "Helps **even out the appearance** of skin tone over time, with consistent use and daily sunscreen" |
| "Cures acne" | "Lightweight and suitable for most skin types" · "Non-comedogenic" **(only with a test report)** |
| "Anti-ageing / reverses wrinkles" | "Helps reduce the **appearance** of fine lines" **(only with efficacy substantiation)** · otherwise: "Hydrated skin looks smoother" |
| "100% natural" | "Made with [named ingredients] · Full INCI list on the pack" |
| "Chemical-free" | "No steroids, no hydroquinone, no mercury — those are prescription drugs or prohibited in cosmetics in India" |
| "No side effects" | "Patch test first. Everyone's skin is different. Not for use on broken or infected skin" |
| "Results in 7 days" | "Realistic timeline: hydration and a fresher look in a few days; softer texture in 2–3 weeks; how your tone looks in 6–12 weeks, **with daily sunscreen**" |
| "Dermatologist tested" | "Here is our full ingredient list and our batch number — check it yourself" (until you hold an actual HRIPT/patch-test report) |
| "India's No. 1" | "Made in Ahmedabad by a small team" |
| "Best night cream" | "A night cream at ₹499 with [X]% niacinamide — here's exactly what's in it" |
| "Guaranteed glow" | "Most people notice their skin feels softer by morning. If yours doesn't, message me" |

### Your five strongest safe claims — use these constantly
1. **"Full ingredient list printed on the pack."** Always true if you print it. Verifiable in ten seconds by any visitor. Devastating differentiator.
2. **"Batch number, manufacturing date and best-before on every unit."** Legally required anyway — so make it a marketing feature.
3. **"No steroids, no hydroquinone, no mercury."** True, verifiable, and directly addresses a real and widely-reported safety problem in Indian skin creams. Your dossier explains exactly why each is excluded.
4. **"Here is what it cannot do."** The single most sendable, most trusted, and most legally safe content you can produce.
5. **"A realistic timeline, not a promise."** Your dossier's honest-expectation table is ready-made content and it is the opposite of everything ASCI is enforcing against.

## Part 4 — Before / after imagery: the rules

Before-and-after is the highest-risk content format in cosmetics advertising, and ASCI's healthcare guidelines §3 has **specific rules on before-and-after imagery**.

### ❌ Never
- AI-generated or digitally altered before/afters. This is fabricated evidence.
- Split-screen implying a **medical** outcome (acne cleared, melasma gone, pigmentation removed).
- Fixed-timeline promises attached to the images ("day 1 vs day 7").
- Lighting, angle, makeup, filter or expression differences between the two frames.
- Someone else's photos presented as your customer's.
- Any before/after from a customer **without written permission**, and never a minor.

### ✅ What you can do instead
- **A usage diary**, clearly labelled as one person's experience, same lighting, same angle, same time of day, no filter, with an explicit on-screen line: *"Individual experience. Not a clinical result. Results vary."*
- **Texture and sensory demonstrations** — how the cream looks on glass, how it spreads, how it absorbs. Zero claim risk, high watch time.
- **Instrumental measurements** if you commission them (corneometer for hydration, chromameter/mexameter for melanin index) — **this is the only route to a genuine efficacy claim**, and your dossier §8D says exactly this.
- **"What changed for me, honestly"** founder content, with the caveats spoken aloud.

## Part 5 — Labelling requirements (photograph your label — it is free trust content)

Under the Cosmetics Rules 2020, a cosmetic label must carry:

- ☐ Product name
- ☐ **Net content** (Legal Metrology)
- ☐ **Full ingredient list in descending order of concentration**
- ☐ **Batch number**
- ☐ **Manufacturing date**
- ☐ **Best-before / expiry**
- ☐ **Manufacturing licence number**
- ☐ **Manufacturer's name and complete address**
- ☐ Directions for use
- ☐ Warnings and cautions
- ☐ **MRP** (Legal Metrology)
- ☐ **Country of origin**
- ☐ Consumer-care contact details

**Marketing move:** publish a carousel called *"How to read any cosmetic label in India"* explaining each of these. It is public-service content, it positions you as the expert, it ranks for `cosmetic label india` and `how to read ingredient list`, and it lets you photograph **your own compliant label** as proof without ever making a claim.

⚠️ For **selling on WhatsApp or Instagram Shops in India, the `origin_country` field is required** in your Meta catalogue — items will not show without it.

## Part 6 — Manufacturing & licensing (the part that decides whether you can sell at all)

Summarised from your own `formulation/day-cream-v1-india-dossier.md` §8A, which is accurate and worth re-reading:

- Cosmetics are regulated under the **Cosmetics Rules 2020**; apply on **Form COS-5** (own manufacture) or **Form COS-6** (loan licence / third-party manufacture); licence granted on **Form COS-8 / COS-9**, typically ~45 days subject to inspection. `[VERIFY — timelines and forms change]`
- Premises must be in a **notified industrial area, not a residential home**, and comply with **GMP requirements of the Seventh Schedule**.
- You must employ **competent technical staff** (Diploma/Degree in Pharmacy registered under the Pharmacy Act 1948, or B.Tech/B.Sc in Cosmetic Technology, or Intermediate with Chemistry as accepted by the SLA).
- **The realistic path for a new brand, per your own dossier: a licensed third-party/contract manufacturer under a loan licence (COS-6)** — *"far cheaper and faster than building a compliant plant. You keep the formula and brand; they hold the GMP infrastructure."*
- Your dossier's §9.8 is unambiguous about home batches: *"Do not give or sell it to others — in India, manufacturing cosmetics for sale or distribution without a licence under the Cosmetics Rules, 2020 is an offence."*

### Testing that substantiates claims (dossier §8D)
| Test | What it lets you say |
|---|---|
| Challenge test / PET (**ISO 11930**) | That your preservative system works. Non-negotiable |
| Microbial limits per batch | Release criterion; supports a "every batch tested" story |
| Stability (3 months at 40 °C/75% RH + 25 °C + 4 °C + freeze-thaw + light) | **Supports your shelf-life claim** |
| Heavy metals per IS 4707 | Compliance |
| Assay of actives (HPLC) at t0 and end of stability | **Proves "4% niacinamide" is true at expiry, not just at filling** |
| HRIPT / dermatologist-supervised patch test (≈50–200 panelists) | **The only thing that lets you say "dermatologically tested"** |
| Efficacy substantiation (corneometer, chromameter/mexameter, sebumeter, 8–12 week consumer study) | **The only way you may legally say "reduces the appearance of dark spots"** |
| Packaging compatibility & transport testing | Prevents field failures |

**Sequencing insight:** you do not need these tests to *start* on Instagram. You need them to *claim*. So start with content that makes zero efficacy claims (ingredients, process, honesty, education, local relevance) and commission testing as revenue allows. Then upgrade your claims one at a time, each backed by a report. That is a legal and commercial advantage over brands that claimed first and panicked later.

## Part 7 — Platform rules that overlap with the law

| Rule | Source | Practical effect |
|---|---|---|
| Cosmetic ads must target **18+**; no statements of inferiority about physical appearance; no permanent skin whitening or bleaching | Meta ad policy | Set your ad targeting to 18+; keep copy free of appearance-inferiority framing |
| Product misrepresentation and misleading pricing are prohibited commercial practices, enforced at **account level** | Meta ad policy | An account-level enforcement action can disable your advertising entirely |
| Advertisements on social media must be **clearly identifiable as promos/ads** | ASCI Code | Disclose any paid partnership. Use Instagram's paid-partnership label |

| Hashtags capped at 5; generic tags can hurt | Instagram, 18 Dec 2025 | Five targeted tags |

## Part 8 — Pre-publish claim check (run this on every post)

Before you publish anything that mentions what your product does:

1. **Is this an objective, verifiable statement?** If no → fine, it is opinion or education.
2. **If yes, can I produce evidence for it today?** If no → rewrite or remove. ASCI can demand substantiation.
3. **Does it imply a medical outcome?** (treats, cures, prevents, removes a condition) → **Remove.**
4. **Does it mention skin colour, fairness, or tone lightening as an improvement?** → **Remove entirely.**
5. **Does it contain a timeline?** → Replace with a range plus "with consistent use and daily sunscreen" plus "individual results vary."
6. **Does it contain a superlative** (best, No. 1, most effective)? → Remove or substantiate.
7. **Does it contain an absolute** (100%, guaranteed, permanent, always, never)? → Remove.
8. **Is there a before/after image?** → Apply Part 4. If in doubt, don't post it.
9. **Would a small-print disclaimer be doing the real work?** → Then the headline claim is the problem. Disclaimers do not rescue unsupportable claims.
10. **If a competitor screenshotted this and sent it to ASCI, would I be comfortable?** → That is the test. If not, rewrite.

## Part 9 — What to do if you receive a complaint or notice

1. **Do not delete the post immediately** — preserve a screenshot and the date. You may need to show what you actually said.
2. **Identify the specific claim** being challenged.
3. **Check whether you hold substantiation.** If yes, gather it. If no, remove or amend the claim promptly — ASCI's code notes that for mass-manufactured goods, lapses within generally acceptable limits may be permitted **if the advertiser takes prompt remedial action**.
4. **Respond in writing, factually, without argument.**
5. **Consult a regulatory professional** before responding to anything from CDSCO, a State Licensing Authority, or the CCPA.
6. **Log it.** Every complaint teaches you which of your claims was too loose. Fix the whole class of claim, not just the one post.

---

## The one-line summary

**Describe what your product is and what it feels like. Never describe what it cures, and never describe a lighter skin tone as a better one.** Publish your ingredients, your percentages, your batch numbers and your limitations — they are simultaneously your safest claims and your strongest marketing.
